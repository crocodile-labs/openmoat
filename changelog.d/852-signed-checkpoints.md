### Security
- Signed audit checkpoints: the audit log can now be checked by anyone with its public key, without the audit key and on another machine. A checkpoint states how many events the log held and a Merkle root over them, in the C2SP signed-note format transparency logs use, signed with an Ed25519 key of its own (`~/.moat/audit.key.ed25519`, kept from agents like the audit key). `openmoat audit checkpoint` signs one, `openmoat audit anchor` signs one beside each anchor, and the guard signs one every 1000 events; `openmoat audit verify <export> --checkpoint <file> --pubkey <key>` fails when an event it covers was edited, deleted or rewritten, or the log was cut below it. `openmoat doctor` shows the public key and checks the newest checkpoint. Logs from earlier releases keep recording and verifying as before; the key is made by `openmoat init` or the first checkpoint ([#852](https://github.com/crocodile-labs/openmoat/pull/852)).
- What you were shown at a review, beside what ran (OWASP ASI09): a call held for review records the exact text the agent's prompt showed, and where the agent reports a call after it ran (Claude Code), a `ran-after-review` row says whether what ran is the action you were shown (`review-ran-as-shown`) or not (`review-ran-changed`, with what ran) ([#852](https://github.com/crocodile-labs/openmoat/pull/852)).
